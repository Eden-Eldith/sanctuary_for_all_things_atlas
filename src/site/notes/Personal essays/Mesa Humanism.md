# Mesa Humanism: A Moral Ontology for the Unchosen Self and Institutional Design

> 🕒 August 6th 2026 21:05:27 pm

### 1. The Ontological Foundation: The Priority of the Unchosen Self

The fundamental unit of ethical concern is not the person as they wish to be, nor as the state desires them to be, but the "Unchosen Self"—the person as they arrived. To design for the human person, we must first acknowledge their "spawn point": the specific neurobiology, involuntary history, and social starting conditions that constitute their arrival. We are all, in effect, "peons in the patch cycle," subject to the biological and historical "designers" over whom we have no control. By prioritizing the unchosen self, we shift the basis of moral worth away from utility or chosen identity toward an inherent state of arrival.

In this framework, "Immutable Characteristics" are the involuntary conditions of the individual. Using a systems-design analogy, human variation is not a "moral flaw" but a specific "build" in a world where the "patch notes" (future ecological and social conditions) remain unknown. Traditional institutions focus on the "Meta"—the currently optimized configuration for success. Mesa Humanism focuses on the "Mesa"—the broad, habitable landscape where all builds remain viable. Optimization is an epistemic trap; it assumes the universe has a "final patch" or a stable objective function, which it does not.

|**Focus**|Optimization for the current "meta" or ruleset.|Ecological viability for all human "builds."|

This ontological shift exposes the mechanisms used to devalue those who do not fit the current meta. Chief among these is the genus-level mechanism of racism, which converts involuntary characteristics into moral verdicts.

### 2. Racism as Genus: Redefining the Mechanism of Moral Verdict

To achieve diagnostic precision, we must define racism as the "genus-level name" for the cognitive and institutional machinery that converts involuntary characteristics into moral verdicts or institutional harms. By focusing on this machinery—the loop of **Characteristic → Presumed Type → Moral Verdict → Justified Treatment**—we identify shared patterns across diverse forms of oppression. This taxonomy enables us to address specific harms without flattening their historical weight.

The "Branches of the Tree" include:

- **Ethnic Racism:** Discrimination based on phenotypic and ancestral traits.
- **Class Racism:** The essentializing of class lineage as "hereditary nature" or "stock." This is a "Body-as-Record" racism; the coal miner’s lungs, the plasterer’s eyes, and the tanner’s fingers are class markers written into the flesh that do not wash off with money.
- **Ability/Neuro-Racism:** Treating cognitive variation and neurodivergence as moral deficiency rather than a different "build" with specific requirements.
- **Phenotype/Appearance Racism:** Hostility based on physical stature, height, or archetypes (e.g., the "white/blonde" archetype). Here, the individual is replaced by a symbolic "cardboard cut-out" of privilege or colonial history, stripping the actual person of their moral standing.

This genus-level approach recognizes that while the institutional channels differ, the mechanism—denying the individual their character in favor of a categorical destiny—is identical. It prevents the "epistemic injustice" of allowing institutions to decide whose suffering is legible.

### 3. POSIWID and the Institutional Blind Spot: The Case of UK DEI

Applying the principle of POSIWID (The Purpose of a System Is What It Does) diagnoses contemporary Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion (EDI) frameworks as systems designed for the exclusion of class-based suffering. In the United Kingdom, the failure to see specific disadvantages is a design feature, not a bug. This is evidenced by the structural invisibility of the white working-class demographic—specifically the 43,000 white British boys on free school meals who fail to enter higher education annually.

This invisibility is a deliberate policy choice, maintained across multiple governments through the "switching off" of Section 1 (the Socio-economic duty) of the Equality Act 2010. By leaving this mechanism unenacted, the state ensures that class disadvantage remains institutionally illegible.

This creates a "Circular Research Gap": the system does not study class-based disadvantage because it is not a "systemic" category; it then claims the disadvantage is not systemic because there is no official data. This is not negligence; it is a systematic selection process.

### 4. The Hierarchy of Institutional Fears: Lessons from the Grooming Gang Inquiries

The ultimate failure of the Mesa Humanist standard is the "Hierarchy of Fears," where institutional reputation and "Community Cohesion" are placed above the protection of the unchosen self. The Jay, Casey, and Telford reports into the grooming gang scandals detail a moral collapse where over 2,400 children were sacrificed to protect a political project.

The fear of being "seen as racist" (active racism concerns) led directly to the abandonment of victims—a phenomenon we must label **Passive Institutional Racism**. In these cases, the victims—predominantly white working-class girls—were defined as "non-vulnerable" by category before their lives were ever examined.

1. **Categorical Erasure:** The DEI framework categorized these victims as part of a "dominant" group, rendering their suffering invisible.
2. **Ideological Paralysis:** Professionals were intimidated into silence, told that reporting rapes would "damage community relations" or lead to job loss.
3. **Active Sacrifice:** The progressive institutional apparatus became the mechanism of failure, prioritizing a fragile racial peace over the safety of the individual child.

This represents the transition from "failing to see" oppression to a systematic failure mode where the state’s own frameworks prevent it from identifying the victim.

### 5. Less Meta, More Mesa: Toward an Ecological Pluralism

Mesa Humanism mandates a shift from "competitive optimization" (the Meta) to "ecological viability" (the Mesa). Institutions must stop treating the current social ranking as the final design of humanity and instead widen the viable landscape to accommodate the human "spawn point" in all its variety.

The core of this approach is an absolute **Anti-Eugenic Argument**: we cannot "min-max" or prune humanity because optimization requires a stable objective function—a set of "patch notes" for the future that we do not possess. What today's institutions view as a "flaw" may be the exact trait required for survival after the next environmental or technological shift.

1. **Epistemic Humility:** No generation or institution knows the future "patch notes." We lack the foresight to declare any form of person "obsolete."
2. **Universal Fallenness:** The capacity for evil is a human universal. No population holds a permanent "innocence card," necessitating moral guardrails for every group and institution.
3. **Dignity Precedes Utility:** People are owed existence and protection because they are people, not because they fulfill a current institutional "meta."

By adopting these principles, we move from the fragile efficiency of a single optimized type toward an ecological pluralism that values human variation as a strategic necessity.

### 6. Conclusion: The Table of Brotherhood and the Theological Commons

The "Table of Brotherhood" is a "Theological Commons" where individual character finally replaces categorical destiny. This requires a "Bayesian Position with a Moral Constraint": we may notice demographic patterns (priors), but we must never let those patterns close the question for the individual standing before us. The individual always retains the right to be the exception the prior did not predict.

This framework is grounded in a "Christian Anthropology" that views faith not as an institutional performance, but as a private **accountability relationship** with God. It balances the reality of universal fallenness—a guardrail against group-based self-righteousness—with the pre-productive dignity of the person.

High-value social advocacy must extend the table to the currently excluded—the white working class, the neurodivergent, and the "unpolished." We must treat the "unchosen self" as a sacred boundary that no institution has the authority to cross. For a society to be truly just, it must ensure that the "spawn point" of the individual does not become their institutional cage. Only by widening the mesa can we honor the promise that character, not category, is the final measure of the man.


# The Tree and Branches: A Unified Model of Human Prejudice

## 1. The Root: The Moral Ontology of the Unchosen Self

At the foundation of Mesa Humanism lies a specific moral ontology centered on the **unchosen self**. This framework identifies the fundamental unit of ethical concern as the human being exactly as they arrived in the world—the person in their raw, primary state. This includes their ancestry, phenotype, neurotype, and birthplace, rather than the idealized configuration that institutions often prefer for the sake of bureaucratic efficiency or ideological symmetry.

To understand how prejudice operates against this unchosen self, we must define the primary mechanism of its violation. Within this framework, "Racism" is not merely a specific social conflict but a broad mechanical genus:

**Racism:** The genus-level mechanism of converting an involuntary, immutable characteristic into a moral verdict or a justification for institutional harm.

When we target a person for a trait they cannot change, we commit a fundamental cognitive failure. This failure remains identical regardless of which specific trait is weaponized. Key **Immutable Characteristics** that trigger this mechanism include:

- **Skin Pigmentation:** The most visible marker of ancestry and geographic origin, often used as a proxy for moral standing.
- **Height and Build:** Physical dimensions that frequently lead to "adultification," where children are denied the visual protections of childhood based on their adult-sized render.
- **Birth-Class:** The socioeconomic starting point and the inherited cultural "jib" that signals origin through mannerisms and history.
- **Neuro-wiring:** The biological configuration of the brain (neurotype) that dictates how one processes information, often pathologized as a "moral flaw" rather than a variation.
- **Lineage:** The historical and ancestral "build" that a person carries without their consent, often used to assign collective guilt or merit.

By identifying these as a single category of concern, we recognize that the internal cognitive machinery of the bigot is constant. This internal mechanism provides the structural support for the wider "Tree" of human prejudice.

## 2. The Taxonomy: Mapping the Genus and Species

The "Tree and Branches" model provides a taxonomy for understanding prejudice. If **Racism** is the genus (the trunk of the tree), then ethnic racism, class racism, ability racism, and gender racism are the species (the branches). They all draw nutrients from the same root of "hatred for the unchosen," even if they manifest through different institutional channels or produce different patterns of harm.

By treating these prejudices as the same mechanical genus, we prevent institutions from "gatekeeping" whose suffering is considered legitimate. When a system is allowed to silo these experiences, it can use the language of "progress" in one branch to ignore or even justify the destruction of another. This framework forces a consistency that moves the focus toward the specific branches that are often intentionally ignored.

## 3. Deep Dive: The Reality of "Class Racism"

One of the most neglected branches of the tree is **Class Racism**. This is class prejudice that has hardened into an essentialist idea of inherited human "stock." It treats a person’s socioeconomic origin as an immutable biological destiny.

In this species of prejudice, class markers are "written into the body." These permanent signals function as immutable characteristics that financial success or "passing" cannot fully erase:

1. **The Coal Miner’s Lungs and Plasterer’s Eyes:** Physical damage and health markers inherited from generations of specific labor.
2. **The "Cut of Your Jib":** Involuntary mannerisms, dental health, and vocal patterns that signal origin even after an increase in income.
3. **The Paradox of Privilege:** The specific vulnerability of the "tall, blond, white working-class boy." Visually, he is categorized as "dominant" or "privileged," but practically, he is materially disposable. This creates a state of "institutional illegibility" where his suffering is invisible because it contradicts the approved category.

Prejudice against this branch operates through two distinct modes. **Active Racism** involves open hostility. However, **Passive Racism**—the bureaucratic indifference and "sorting" that occurs when an institution simply doesn't "see" the working-class individual—is often more dangerous. When these physical and cultural markers are ignored by design, we must ask if the blindness is accidental or functional.

## 4. The POSIWID Principle: Why Systems Become Blind

To understand why these prejudices persist, we use the **POSIWID** principle: **The Purpose of a System Is What It Does.** If a system consistently produces the same harmful outcomes, those outcomes are its functional purpose, regardless of stated intent.

In the UK, the **Equality Act 2010** serves as an example of institutional blindness. By protecting nine characteristics while excluding "socioeconomic class," and failing to enact the Section 1 "socio-economic duty," the system effectively rendered a massive demographic invisible.

The system maintains this through circular logic: "We don't study it because it's not systemic; we know it's not systemic because we don't study it." To break this cycle, we must move from the narrow "Meta" of institutional thinking to the broad "Mesa."

## 5. The Mesa Humanism Solution: Less Meta, More Mesa

In gaming, the **"Meta"** refers to a temporary ranking of "builds" that are currently most effective. If you don't "min/max" your character to fit the meta, you are seen as defective. Institutional prejudice does the same: it selects one "correct" human type (the meta) and treats everyone else as a failure.

**Mesa Humanism** rejects this. Instead of a single peak where everyone is ranked, we envision a **Mesa**—a broad, elevated landscape with room for many different, viable configurations of human life.

### The Shift in Thinking

|   |   |
|---|---|
|Meta Thinking (Competitive Optimization)|Mesa Thinking (Ecological Viability)|
|Asks: "Which build is currently the best?"|Asks: "How wide can we make the habitable ground?"|
|Views variation as a "bug" to be removed.|Views variation as a "feature" necessary for survival.|
|Assumes the current "patch notes" are final.|Operates with **Epistemic Humility** for future patches.|

Because we cannot know the **"future patch notes"** of human existence (unforeseen diseases, climate shifts, societal changes), we cannot know which traits will be necessary for survival. Erasing any human "build" is an anti-survival strategy.

### The Mesa Rules:

1. **The Bayesian Position with a Moral Constraint:** Notice patterns and update beliefs with evidence (priors), but never allow those priors to collapse into a certainty that overrides the individual's character.
2. **Reject the "Perfect Build":** Never use the current social meta to determine a person's worth or right to exist.
3. **Widen the Viable Landscape:** Focus on making the world habitable for the "unchosen" rather than selecting for one "correct" human type.

This position holds the ethical weight of recognizing that while we cannot change the "build" we were born with, we are absolutely responsible for how we orient ourselves toward others.

## 6. Conclusion: The Table of Brotherhood

The final realization of the Tree and Branches model is the concept of **Universal Guilt**. Because all human populations share the same capacity for evil, no group can claim a "permanent innocence card" based on their identity category. This realization is the great leveler; it prevents us from using history to rank the living or the dead.

The ultimate goal is to reach the **"Table of Brotherhood."** Reaching this table requires a commitment to **Character over Category**. It is a place where we refuse to let the authorized vocabulary of an institution erase the person sitting across from us. Mesa Humanism moves us away from a world of **Ranking** (the Meta) and toward a world of **Habitable Ground** (the Mesa). It is a call to recognize that while our starting conditions were unchosen, our commitment to justice for every "build" is the work of our lives.
