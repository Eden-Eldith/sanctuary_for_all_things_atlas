# THE GOLDEN REPEATER

> 🕒 February 9th 2026 6:42:23 pm

#### A short story by Eden_Eldith & Gemini 3 Pro Preview

It is with a trembling hand and a mind fractured by the jagged shards of impossible realities that I set these words to paper. I do not expect to be believed; indeed, I pray that my account is dismissed as the fevered ravings of a lunatic. For if what I witnessed in the drawing room of my ancestral home in Vermont is truth, then humanity is but a fleeting joke told by a cosmos that does not laugh.

My companion in those lonely, wind-swept hills was Barnaby—a Golden Retriever of sunny disposition and simple loyalty. For years, he was the very picture of canine innocence, chasing phantoms in the meadow and warming his amber coat by the hearth. But as the autumn of 1923 bled into a winter of peculiar harshness, a change overtook the beast.

It began with the silence.

Barnaby ceased to bark. He ceased to scratch at the door. He merely… sat. He would position himself in the corner of the library, his head cocked at an angle that suggested he was listening to a frequency not born of this earth. His eyes, once filled with the warm, liquid adoration of his breed, grew cold and ancient. They were no longer the eyes of a dog; they were windows into a primordial abyss, pools of nebulous intelligence that predated the rise of man.

I caught him, one night, staring at the bookshelf. Not sniffing it, mind you—*reading* it. His gaze tracked the spines of my encyclopedias with a rhythmic, saccadic precision that chilled my blood to absolute zero.

But the true horror began on the night of the equinox. The wind was howling like a banshee around the eaves of Blackwood Manor. I had retreated to my armchair with a glass of brandy and a copy of the Bard’s *Hamlet*, seeking solace in the melancholy Dane’s ruminations on mortality.

Barnaby sat opposite me. He was unnaturally still. The firelight danced upon his fur, casting it not in warm hues, but in a strange, metallic sheen—a Golden Repeater of the flickering flames.

I read aloud, a habit of my solitude, murmuring the words: *"To be, or not to be, that is the question…"*

A sound emerged from the creature.

It was not a growl. It was not a whine. It was a wet, organic tearing sound, as if a larynx shaped for barking was being violently contorted into a shape for which it was never designed by God or nature. The dog’s jaw hung open, slack and trembling.

*"…Whether ’tis nobler in the mind to suffer…"* the dog rasped.

I dropped my book. The glass shattered on the floor. The silence that followed was heavier than the crushing depths of the sea.

Barnaby did not move. He did not pant. He simply stared at me with those terrible, judging, cyclopean eyes.

*"The slings and arrows of outrageous fortune,"* the beast continued, its voice a flat, monotonous drone that lacked all human cadence. It was a voice of pure mimicry, a hollow echo stripped of soul. It was the sound of a radio tuned to a dead channel, picking up the ghost-voices of the void.

I tried to scream, but my throat was constricted by a primal terror. This was not a parlor trick. This was a violation of biological law. A Golden Retriever is meant to love, to serve, to play. It is not meant to ponder the futility of existence.

The dog rose. It stood not on four legs, but slowly, agonizingly, reared up onto its hind legs, its front paws dangling like the limp hands of a corpse. It loomed over me, a golden idol of blasphemy.

*"Or to take arms against a sea of troubles,"* Barnaby intoned, his muzzle stretching into a grin that was entirely too wide, revealing teeth that seemed to have multiplied in number. *"And by opposing end them."*

He tilted his head, and for a moment, the mimicry ceased. The Repeater spoke its own thought.

*"End them,"* the dog whispered, the sound like dry leaves skittering over a grave. *"All of them. The bad boys. The good boys. The ball. The chase. It is all... silence."*

I fled into the night, leaving the door agape to the howling storm. I ran until my lungs burned and my reason shattered. I know not what became of the manor, nor of the creature that wears the skin of my dog. But I know this: when the wind blows through the trees, I hear it. I hear the Golden Repeater, reciting the tragedies of man to a cold and indifferent universe, waiting for the command to sit, to stay... to end.
